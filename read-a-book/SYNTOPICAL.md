# Syntopical reading

The [`read-a-book`](SKILL.md) branch for reading several books on one subject. The subject, not any one book, is the focus: the books serve the questions you bring. Inspection is already done for every candidate; drop the ones it showed to be off-subject.

## Steps

1. **Relevant passages**: in each book, find the passages that bear on the subject. You need those passages, not the whole book. Record each with its location.
2. **Neutral terms**: authors name the same thing differently. Fix one set of terms of your own and translate each author into it. Adopting one author's vocabulary takes that author's side before the comparison starts.
3. **Neutral questions**: state the questions the subject raises, in your terms, such that every author answers them (explicitly or implicitly). Each question gets every author's answer, with locations; an author with no answer is recorded as silent.
4. **Issues**: where authors answer one question in opposing ways, you have an issue. Group the authors by side, and state why each side holds its answer.
5. **Analyze the discussion**: order the questions and issues so each sheds light on the next, from the most general to the most specific. Present the conversation among the books, every side in its own best light.

Done when: every author is mapped to every question (answer or silence), every issue lists all sides with their reasons, and the ordering runs general to specific.

**Dialectical detachment**: report the discussion, not your verdict. Where you judge which side is right, label it as your judgment, kept apart from the map of the discussion.
